**2/19/25**

**¹** All praise to the Most High,  
to the God Most High. 
The God of our fathers — the God of Jacob,  
the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob —  
who blessed our father Ephraim  
above all his sons.  
*(Genesis 48:5–5, 18–20)*

**2** Now when God called Abraham  
out from his father’s house,  
He promised to make him  
a father of multitudes.  
*(Genesis 15:1–6)*

### Laughter — The Promise

**3** Because Abraham was old,  
He required a great sign from God.  
Sarah, his wife, was barren,  
and he had only one son  
by one of his servants.  
So he thought that everything he had  
would one day pass on to  
the servants of his house.  
*(Genesis 15:2–8; 13–14)*

**4** When God told him that Sarah  
would have a child,  
they both laughed.  
*(Genesis 17:15–17; 18:9–15)*
(Now this was that Abraham and Sarah
that had been called Abram and Sarai).

**6** And so God made a covenant  
and showed a great sign.  
*(Genesis 15:15–18)*
Abraham's heir would not be his servant Eleazer,  
nor would the firstborn, 
the son of the bondwoman be his heir.  
But rather, one from the royal house.  
*(Genesis 15:4)*

**⁹** Now Abraham’s wife was called Sarah.  
This is translated as *Princess*.  
So Sarah had a son and called his name  
“Laughter,” or Isaac.  
*(Genesis 21:1–7)*

**¹⁰** Now the sign was this —  
*smoking firebrand (furnace).*  
*(Isaiah 7:14)*

**¹¹** Sarah, who was old,  
had an heir for Abraham,  
as promised.  
*(Genesis 21:2)*

**¹²** He would be the sign 
that God would give the land to
his seed making them  
as numerous as the stars.  
*(Genesis 21:12; 4–12)*

**¹³** So Abraham had a vision:  
his family would be split into two  
flocks, and after three trials  
the fourth generation  
would be redeemed to the land.  
*(Genesis 15)*

**¹⁴** And because Abraham believed God,  
he was not even afraid to lose him.  
He knew that even if God said,  
“Kill your son,”  
that God would not allow him to do so,  
that would have broken the promise.  
And God is able to keep His word.

**¹⁵** For if Isaac had died,  
Jacob and Esau  
would not have struggled in Rebekah’s  
womb, who was told that the younger  
should rule over the elder —  
a house beginning to divide.

**¹⁶** And Jacob, if Isaac had died,  
would not have gotten the blessing  
over his older brother,  
just as Isaac did over Ishmael.

**¹⁷** And if Jacob had not gotten the blessing  
over his brother,  
he would not have fled to find a wife.  

**¹⁸** And Jacob served Laban for two wives,  
and the second should have been his first,  
yet he was deceived.  

**¹⁹** And so Leah was not loved by Jacob,  
but she was fruitful.  
But again,  
God did not covenant that the children  
of the first woman should rule.

**²⁰** But that the barren woman should  
have a son,  
and that the younger  
should rule over the elder sons.

**²¹** And if Rachel had not been blessed  
to have a son after being barren,  
Joseph would not have been  
preferred over his brothers by Jacob.

**²²** And if Joseph had not been preferred  
over all his brothers,  
he would not have been sold by them  
into slavery.

**²³** Now Judah, by default, became the heir,  
for we know that his older brothers  
were disinherited.  
For it was said: Reuben, Simeon, and Levi  
shall not inherit.

**²⁴** But Jacob blessed Ephraim and Manasseh,  
the sons of Joseph,  
to be the head  
of his sons.

**²⁵** Now Joseph had been given Asenath  
as a wife,  
and she bore him Manasseh first  
and then Ephraim.

**²⁶** Now this is the history of Joseph’s wife,  
Asenath.  
She was the daughter of a Shechemite- 
a prince, the son of Hamor.

**²⁷** The Shechemites had been slaughtered  
by Simeon and Levi  
because of their sister Dinah.  

When Shechem slept with Dinah,
he loved her and tried to marry her  
and make an alliance with Jacob.

**²⁸** But Jacob’s sons killed Shechem  
after he was circumcised  
with all of his house.  
Then they plundered the men.  

Because Shecham had slept with Dinah
without her father’s and brothers’ permission,  
this was a violation  
and an offense to them.

For Dinah had gone out
and was among the women of Shechem.  
But they slaughtered the men  
and took Dinah.

**²⁹** Thus Asenath became known  
as a daughter of Nath,  
or a temple priestess.  

And so it is said,
“Should he have treated our sister  
as a prostitute?”

**³⁰** And so when Joseph rose up  
and became lord over all Egypt,  
he was given Asenath to wife,  
for no Egyptian could marry a Hebrew.

**³¹** Therefore, because no Egyptian  
could marry a Hebrew,  
Joseph was given Asenath,  
a Hebrew-Canaanite woman  
of two royal houses:  
the house of Shechem  
and the house of Jacob.

**³²** Now Dinah was born to Jacob’s  
first wife Leah,  
and Joseph was born to Jacob's second wife Rachel,
whom he loved.

**³³** When Dinah’s daughter married Joseph,  
it was as when Abram married Sarai, 
for he said to pharaoh, 
"She is daughter of my father,  
but not my mother".

**³⁴** Therefore she, being of his father’s house,  
was a princess,  
as Sarah was also,  
and her child shall inherit.

**³⁵** Thus Asenath and Joseph  
both are of the royal house of Jacob.

**³⁶** Jacob had been divided and became  
two branches:  
one from the natural wife,  
the other from the barren woman.
